It started with a dream.

A small boat. A quiet lake. Bella sitting up front with her nose in the wind, and me at the back with one hand on the tiller, not going anywhere in particular and in no hurry to get there.

That was the whole dream. Nothing happened in it. No storm, no sea monster, no message from the beyond.

It just felt *good*. The kind of good that's still there when you wake up, sitting on your chest like a cat.

Most dreams have the decency to fade by breakfast. This one didn't. By lunch I was on Facebook Marketplace, looking at sailboats. I kept at it for a couple of weeks, scrolling past boats that were too big, too broken, or too far gone, until one finally looked like it would work.

In Sacramento.

Which is not, I'd like to point out, next door. But dreams don't account for geography, and apparently neither do I. So I drove down, handed over the money, hitched it up, and towed my new old boat home like a man who had made a perfectly reasonable decision.

Then came the part the dream skipped. Lines and fittings and figuring out what goes where. Every piece made sense on its own. Together they made a puzzle, and I'd lost the box with the picture on it.

Eventually it all came together. More or less. Enough.

I took it up to the lake and put it in.

The first trip out was just me. Somebody had to find out whether this boat floated, and it didn't seem fair to make Bella the test pilot.

It floated. I sailed. Nobody drowned.

Then it was Bella's turn.

Bella is a shepherd, and shepherds take their work seriously. Her work, as far as she's concerned, is doing whatever I ask. So when I asked her to jump in the boat, she jumped in the boat. A little look first, maybe — *you're sure about this?* — but in she went.

I fed her favorite treats. A lot of them. Bribery works on dogs the same way it works on everybody.

Then I waded in knee-deep and pushed the boat around a little, slowly, Bella riding on board while I walked beside her in the cold water. She watched the shore move. She watched me. She decided this was fine.

So I climbed in, and we took off.

And there it was. The dream. Bella up front, nose in the wind. Me at the back, hand on the tiller. Going nowhere in particular.

Here's what you have to understand about Bella. She loves the water. She'll swim until you make her stop. But she had never been *on* the water — floating over it, staying dry, watching the lake slide by underneath like a slow movie.

I think it rearranged something in her.

On the next trip she was in the boat before I'd finished unloading the car. On the trip after that she sat a little closer to the tiller. She watched my hands. When the wind shifted, she looked at the sail before I did.

I told myself she was just being a good dog. Paying attention. That's what shepherds do.

Shepherds, it turns out, also plan ahead.

I turned my back for a minute. One minute. I was fussing with something on shore, a line or a cooler, I honestly don't remember. When I turned around, the boat was not where I'd left it.

It was out on the lake.

With Bella in it.

By herself.

Sail full, ears up, heading for the far shore like she'd been doing it her whole life. She didn't look back. She didn't need to. She'd had a dream of her own, apparently, and had only been waiting for me to get out of the way.

[![](/images/dreamboat-bella-solo.webp)](/images/dreamboat-bella-solo.webp)

I thought I'd bought that boat for my dream.

Turns out it was her dream, too.
